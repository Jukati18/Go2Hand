## 1. Why Grading Matters

Accurate grading is the single most important factor in Go2Hand's trust model. It protects everyone involved:

**For sellers:**
- Accurate listings generate fewer disputes and better reviews
- Honest grading builds a reputation that commands premium prices on future listings
- Over-grading (listing a B as an A) leads to buyer disputes, forced returns, and negative ratings that persist on your profile

**For buyers:**
- Clear grading standards let you compare devices across different sellers with confidence
- You can make purchasing decisions knowing exactly what condition "Grade B" means — not just one seller's interpretation

**For the platform:**
- Consistent grading standards allow Go2Hand to enforce its buyer protection policies fairly
- Dispute resolution is faster and clearer when both parties understand the agreed-upon grading definition

> **The core principle:** Grade the device as it *is*, not as you wish it were. A buyer who receives a device better than expected leaves five stars. A buyer who receives a device worse than described opens a dispute.

## 2. The Four Grades at a Glance

| Grade | Label | Typical Use | Battery Health | Cosmetic Condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A+** | Like New | Barely used or unused | 95%+ | Zero visible wear |
| **A** | Excellent | Light regular use | 85–95% | Micro-marks only under close inspection |
| **B** | Good | Normal everyday use | 75–85% | Visible light scratches, no screen damage |
| **C** | Fair | Heavy or long-term use | 65–80% | Noticeable wear, possible dents |

> **Important:** These grades describe **cosmetic condition only**. All devices listed on Go2Hand must be **fully functional** regardless of grade. Any functional faults must be explicitly disclosed in the description as a separate matter.

## 3. Grade A+ — Like New

### Definition

A Grade A+ device appears as if it has never been used or has been used only a handful of times in ideal conditions. The cosmetic state is indistinguishable from new retail stock.

### Criteria (all must be true)

**Screen:**
- ✅ Zero scratches, marks, or blemishes — verified under bright light at multiple angles
- ✅ No dead pixels, no screen burn-in, no colour irregularities
- ✅ Original screen protector may still be present (but is not required)

**Body / Chassis:**
- ✅ Zero scratches on back panel under any lighting
- ✅ Zero scuffs on corners, sides, or edges
- ✅ No dents, bends, or gaps in the chassis
- ✅ All paint, anodising, or finish intact — no chips or peeling

**Ports & Buttons:**
- ✅ All ports clean and undamaged (USB-C, Lightning, headphone jack, SIM tray slot)
- ✅ All physical buttons click firmly with no wobble
- ✅ Camera lenses scratch-free and unfogged

**Functionality:**
- ✅ All features working perfectly — face ID, fingerprint sensor, cellular, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, GPS
- ✅ Speakers and microphones clear with no distortion
- ✅ All sensors functional (proximity, gyroscope, accelerometer)

**Battery:**
- ✅ 95% health or above (iPhone standard)
- ✅ Original battery — no replacement

**Accessories / Packaging:**
- ✅ Ideally includes original box, all inserts, and original accessories
- ✅ Listings without original packaging can still be A+ if all cosmetic criteria are met

### What A+ is not

- A device with any visible marks, even ones that "only appear in direct sunlight"
- A device with a replaced screen, battery, or any other component — even if the work was done by Apple / Samsung / authorised repair
- A device with any previous repair history, regardless of outcome

### Pricing context

A+ devices typically sell at **12–25% below retail price**. Buyers paying A+ prices expect a near-retail experience in terms of appearance.

## 4. Grade A — Excellent

### Definition

A Grade A device shows light signs of careful, normal use. Any cosmetic wear is minor enough that it requires deliberate, close inspection to notice — it does not affect day-to-day use or appearance in normal lighting.

### Criteria

**Screen:**
- ✅ Functionally perfect — no dead pixels, burn-in, or colour issues
- ✅ May have very faint surface micro-scratches visible only under direct artificial light at certain angles — NOT visible from 30+ cm in normal use
- ❌ No visible scratches in standard room lighting
- ❌ No deep scratches reaching the glass surface

**Body / Chassis:**
- ✅ May have very faint micro-scratches on the back or sides — visible only under close inspection with bright light
- ✅ Corners may show the very beginning of anodising wear — a subtle lightening only
- ❌ No visible scratches to the naked eye in normal lighting
- ❌ No scuffs, gouges, or marks that catch the finger when you run your fingernail across them

**Ports & Buttons:**
- ✅ All ports clean and fully functional
- ✅ Buttons firm with no wobble
- ✅ Camera lenses may have extremely light surface cleaning marks (not scratches)

**Functionality:**
- ✅ All features working correctly
- ✅ All sensors operational

**Battery:**
- ✅ 85–95% health
- ✅ Original battery preferred; replacement acceptable if disclosed

### How to test for A vs B

The **fingernail test:** Run your fingernail across the back and screen. On an A-grade device, your fingernail slides smoothly with no catch. If you feel a catch — even a small one — the scratch is deep enough to be Grade B.

The **30 cm test:** Hold the device 30 cm away in normal room lighting. Look at the screen and back. If you can see scratches from this distance without specifically hunting for them, it is Grade B.

### Pricing context

A devices typically sell at **25–40% below retail**. This is the most common grade on Go2Hand and represents good value for buyers.

## 5. Grade B — Good

### Definition

A Grade B device shows normal, visible wear consistent with regular everyday use. Cosmetic marks are present and visible to the naked eye in normal lighting, but they do not affect functionality, and the screen remains crack-free.

### Criteria

**Screen:**
- ✅ Functionally perfect
- ✅ Surface scratches may be visible in normal lighting — light, surface-level only
- ❌ No deep scratches that catch the fingernail on the screen surface
- ❌ No cracks of any kind — even hairline cracks disqualify a device from any grade without explicit disclosure
- ❌ No screen burn-in or dead pixels

**Body / Chassis:**
- ✅ Visible light scratches on back panel in normal lighting
- ✅ Corner wear visible — anodising or finish worn through, showing base material underneath
- ✅ Minor scuffs on sides or edges
- ✅ May have minor dents that do not create a gap in the chassis or affect button operation
- ❌ No bends or warping
- ❌ No cracks in the chassis

**Ports & Buttons:**
- ✅ All ports functional — may show wear cosmetically but do not affect charging or data transfer
- ✅ All buttons fully functional

**Functionality:**
- ✅ All core features working
- ⚠️ Any minor functional quirks must be disclosed (e.g. "Wi-Fi occasionally requires reconnection" — this would normally prevent Grade B, but if disclosed and priced accordingly, it is acceptable)

**Battery:**
- ✅ 75–85% health
- ✅ Replacement battery acceptable if disclosed

### Describing B-grade wear accurately

Use specific language in your listing description rather than vague terms:

| Vague (avoid) | Specific (use this) |
|---|---|
| "Some marks" | "Light scratches visible on back panel in direct light" |
| "Corner wear" | "Anodising worn at two corners showing silver aluminium beneath" |
| "Minor scuffs" | "3–4 scuff marks on right side, approximately 1–2mm each" |

### Pricing context

B devices typically sell at **38–55% below retail**. Buyers accept visible wear in exchange for a meaningfully lower price.

## 6. Grade C — Fair

### Definition

A Grade C device shows heavy or long-term use. Cosmetic wear is significant and immediately apparent. Despite this, all core functionality must work correctly, and the screen must remain crack-free.

### Criteria

**Screen:**
- ✅ Functionally correct — no dead pixels, burn-in, or colour distortion
- ✅ May have multiple visible scratches
- ❌ No cracks — cracks require explicit disclosure and appropriate pricing regardless of other condition

**Body / Chassis:**
- ✅ Significant visible scratches and scuffs on back, sides, and corners
- ✅ Heavy corner wear with underlying material exposed across multiple points
- ✅ May have small dents — must not create a chassis gap, must not affect any port or button function
- ✅ Paint chips on painted finishes

**Ports & Buttons:**
- ✅ All ports functional
- ✅ All buttons must work, though they may feel different from new (e.g. volume rocker slightly stiffer)

**Functionality:**
- ✅ All core features working — calls, SMS, data, camera, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth
- ✅ Charging works correctly
- ✅ Biometrics (face ID / fingerprint) working

**Battery:**
- ✅ 65–80% health
- ✅ Replacement battery likely; disclose if known

### What Grade C is not

Grade C does not mean "broken" or "parts only." If a device does not function correctly across its core features, it cannot be listed on Go2Hand under any grade — it must be sold as "for parts / repair" and clearly labelled as such.

Devices with cracked screens can still be listed, but the crack **must be explicitly disclosed** regardless of what other grade the device might otherwise achieve. A cracked screen affects pricing but does not automatically prevent listing, provided the screen remains functional.

### Pricing context

C devices typically sell at **52–70% below retail**. They serve budget-conscious buyers who prioritise functionality over cosmetics.

## 7. How to Inspect Your Device

Follow this systematic inspection process before grading any device.

### Equipment you'll need

- A bright LED flashlight or use your phone's torch
- A clean, lint-free microfibre cloth
- A well-lit room (natural daylight works best)
- Your fingernail (for scratch depth testing)

### Step-by-step inspection

**Step 1 — Clean the device**  
Wipe the screen and back thoroughly with a microfibre cloth. Dust and fingerprints can disguise or exaggerate scratches. Do not inspect a dirty device.

**Step 2 — Screen inspection (3 steps)**

1. Turn the screen on and display a solid white image. Look for dead pixels (tiny black dots), burn-in (ghost images from previous content), and colour irregularities.
2. Turn the screen off. Hold the device under your LED torch at a 20° angle to the surface. Look for scratches — this angle catches marks that are otherwise invisible.
3. Run your fingernail gently across the screen surface. Any scratch that your nail catches is deep enough to be Grade B or below.

**Step 3 — Back panel inspection**  
Hold the device under your LED torch at multiple angles (20°, 45°, 90°). Look for scratches, scuffs, and cloudiness in the finish. Mark the count and severity.

**Step 4 — Edge and corner inspection**  
Examine every corner and all four sides under the torch. Note any anodising wear (colour change), scuffs, or dents. Run your finger along each edge to feel for any raised metal or dings.

**Step 5 — Port and button inspection**  
Shine the torch into each port. Look for debris, corrosion, or bent pins. Press each button — it should click firmly with no lateral wobble or stickiness.

**Step 6 — Camera lens inspection**  
Hold each camera lens near the torch. Rotate slowly. Look for scratches on the sapphire lens, fog inside the lens housing (condensation damage), or chips around the lens ring.

**Step 7 — Functional test**

Run through this checklist:
- [ ] Make a phone call (earpiece and microphone)
- [ ] Speaker phone (loudspeaker and microphone)
- [ ] Front camera preview
- [ ] Rear camera(s) — photo and video
- [ ] Wi-Fi connects and loads a webpage
- [ ] Bluetooth pairs to another device
- [ ] Charging — plug in and confirm charging icon appears
- [ ] Face ID or fingerprint sensor unlocks the device
- [ ] Volume up, volume down, mute/ring switch
- [ ] Power button
- [ ] GPS — open Maps and confirm location
- [ ] NFC (if applicable)
- [ ] Accelerometer — rotate device and confirm screen auto-rotates

## 8. Battery Health Standards

Battery health is one of the most searched-for specifications on Go2Hand, particularly for smartphones. Report it accurately — battery degradation is measurable and buyers may test it on receipt.

### How to find battery health

**iPhone:**  
Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging → Maximum Capacity

**Android (Samsung):**  
Settings → Battery → Battery Usage → More battery settings  
*(For a precise reading, use the free app AccuBattery after a full charge/discharge cycle)*

**MacBook:**  
Apple Menu → About This Mac → System Report → Power → Full Charge Capacity vs Design Capacity  
Or: Install the free app coconutBattery for a cleaner readout.

### Battery health thresholds by grade

| Battery Health | Assessment | Appropriate Grade |
|---|---|---|
| 95–100% | Near-new condition | A+ or A |
| 85–94% | Good, normal degradation | A |
| 75–84% | Moderate degradation | B |
| 65–74% | Noticeable degradation | C |
| Below 65% | Significant degradation — must be disclosed | C with explicit disclosure |

> **Note:** iPhone shows "Service Recommended" at 79% or below. If this message is showing, the grade cannot be higher than C regardless of cosmetic condition.

### Original vs replacement battery

Always disclose if the battery has been replaced. A replaced battery affects buyer perception even if the health percentage is high.

Use this language in your description:
- **Original battery:** No disclosure needed (it is assumed)
- **Replaced battery:** *"Battery was replaced by [Apple Authorised Service / Samsung Service Centre / third-party repair] in [approximate date/year]. Battery health is currently [X]%."*
- **Unknown battery status:** *"Battery history unknown (purchased second-hand). Current health is [X]%."*

## 9. Screen Assessment Guide

Screens are the most scrutinised component of any device. Use this guide for consistent assessment.

### Scratch severity classification

| Severity | Description | Fingernail Test | Grade Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **None** | Zero marks visible under torch and direct inspection | Smooth — no catch | A+ possible |
| **Micro** | Faint marks visible only under torch at 20° angle, not in regular use | Smooth — no catch | A possible |
| **Light** | Marks visible in room lighting under deliberate inspection | Slightly detectable catch | B |
| **Moderate** | Marks clearly visible to casual inspection | Definite catch | B or C |
| **Heavy** | Multiple deep marks immediately obvious | Strong catch | C |
| **Cracked** | Any crack, even hairline — must be explicitly disclosed | N/A | Mandatory disclosure |

### Dead pixel testing

Display a solid white screen image (open a white webpage or document). In a darkened room:
- **Dead pixels** appear as small black dots that do not change with the image
- **Stuck pixels** appear as tiny coloured dots (red, green, blue) that remain constant
- **Hot pixels** appear as bright white dots

**Any dead or stuck pixels must be disclosed.** Devices with dead pixels in the centre of the screen should not be listed above Grade C. Pixels at extreme screen edges may be disclosed and listed at Grade B with a reduced price.

### Burn-in testing

Display a solid grey image (approximately 50% brightness). Look for ghost outlines of previous content (status bar, navigation bar, icons). This most commonly affects OLED displays used in high-brightness settings.

**Any burn-in must be disclosed.** Significant burn-in affecting the central screen area prevents listing above Grade C.

### Screen touch responsiveness

Test every corner and edge with a drawing app. Areas of the screen that do not respond to touch, or that respond erratically, are a functional fault requiring explicit disclosure regardless of grade.

## 10. Mandatory Disclosures (All Grades)

The following defects **must** be disclosed in your listing description regardless of what grade the device achieves. Failure to disclose these items gives the buyer valid grounds for a dispute and full refund.

### Physical defects requiring disclosure

| Defect | Example disclosure language |
|---|---|
| **Screen crack** (any size) | *"Hairline crack on bottom-left corner of screen, approximately 3cm long. Screen is fully functional — no touch issues."* |
| **Screen burn-in** | *"Faint burn-in of navigation bar visible on grey/light backgrounds at full brightness."* |
| **Dead / stuck pixels** | *"2 dead pixels in top-right corner of screen, approximately 5mm from edge."* |
| **Body crack** | *"Hairline crack on rear glass panel near top edge. No structural compromise."* |
| **Dent affecting chassis gap** | *"Small dent on bottom-left corner creating a 0.5mm gap between chassis and rear panel."* |
| **Non-original screen** | *"Screen was replaced by [repairer] in [approximate timeframe]. Face ID [works / does not work — note: non-Apple screens may affect Face ID]."* |
| **Non-original battery** | *"Battery replaced by [repairer]. Current health [X]%."* |
| **Non-original rear glass** | *"Rear glass replaced. No impact on functionality."* |
| **Water damage history** | *"Device was briefly exposed to water [timeframe ago]. Dried fully, all functions tested and working. Internal damage not confirmed but cannot be ruled out."* |
| **Missing components** | *"SIM tray pin not included. No original box or accessories."* |

### Functional defects requiring disclosure

| Defect | Example disclosure language |
|---|---|
| **Face ID / Touch ID not working** | *"Face ID not functioning — screen was replaced by a third party. Passcode unlock works normally."* |
| **Camera fault** | *"Ultrawide camera has a focus issue in very low light — standard and telephoto cameras work perfectly."* |
| **Intermittent charging** | *"Charging port occasionally requires repositioning of cable. Works reliably with most cables tested."* |
| **Speaker issue** | *"Earpiece speaker has slight crackling at very high volumes — functions normally at normal listening levels."* |
| **Cellular issues** | *"Device is locked to [carrier name]. Unlocking not attempted."* |
| **Wi-Fi or Bluetooth** | *"Wi-Fi drops occasionally and requires toggling Wi-Fi off and on to reconnect. Tested on multiple networks."* |
| **Known software issue** | *"Running iOS 16 — cannot be updated to iOS 17 or above due to [reason]."* |

> **Rule of thumb:** If you noticed it during your ownership, disclose it. If you only discovered it during inspection for this sale, disclose it. If you are unsure whether it counts as a defect, disclose it. A buyer who discovers something you didn't disclose will feel deceived. A buyer who reads your disclosure and decides to purchase anyway has no grounds for a dispute.

## 11. Grade by Device Type

Different device categories have different wear patterns. Here is what to look for and prioritise by type.

### Smartphones

**Primary inspection points:**
- Screen (most critical — largest visible surface)
- Back panel
- Camera bump and lenses
- Corners and sides
- Charging port

**Common wear patterns:**
- Screen micro-scratches from face-down placement
- Back glass scratches and chips (especially without case)
- Camera bump scratches (often the first area to show wear)
- Corner anodising wear from drops
- Charging port lint accumulation (functional, not cosmetic)

**Grade-defining element:** The screen. A pristine back and worn screen cannot achieve Grade A. A worn back and pristine screen can still achieve Grade A.

---

### Laptops

**Primary inspection points:**
- Display panel (inside lid)
- Lid exterior
- Palmrest area
- Keyboard
- Trackpad
- Ports
- Bottom panel (often shows desk wear)

**Common wear patterns:**
- Palmrest polish / anodising wear from extended use (very common on MacBooks)
- Key legends fading on heavily used keyboards
- Trackpad light scratches from ring or watch contact
- Hinge stiffness changes (disclose if different from new)
- Port area scratches from cable insertion

**Grade-defining element:** The display panel and keyboard condition. Palmrest wear is common and can be higher on the cosmetic impact scale — be specific in your description.

**Laptop-specific disclosures:**
- Keyboard key issues (sticky, missing legends, non-functioning keys)
- Hinge tightness (loose hinges are a structural concern)
- Fan noise (excessive fan noise may indicate thermal paste or fan bearing issues)
- Trackpad click not working (software vs hardware)
- Any thermal throttling or overheating history

---

### Tablets

**Primary inspection points:**
- Screen (largest proportion of the device)
- Rear panel
- Corners and edges
- Charging port
- Pencil / stylus connector (if applicable)

**Common wear patterns:**
- Screen micro-scratches (tablets are often used without screen protectors)
- Corner wear from drops
- Rear scratches from use without case

**iPad-specific disclosures:**
- Smart Connector damage or corrosion
- Apple Pencil port issues
- Cellular model vs Wi-Fi only (must be accurate in listing)

---

### Smartwatches

**Primary inspection points:**
- Display / sapphire crystal
- Case body
- Band lugs
- Crown and buttons
- Back plate (heart rate sensor area)

**Common wear patterns:**
- Display micro-scratches (most common)
- Case edge scratches from contact with hard surfaces
- Band lug scratches from band changes
- Crown scratching

**Watch-specific disclosures:**
- Water exposure history (beyond rated water resistance)
- Original band vs third-party band
- Cracked crown
- Sensor accuracy issues (heart rate, blood oxygen)

---

### Audio Devices (AirPods, Headphones, Earbuds)

**Primary inspection points:**
- Driver housing
- Eartips / ear cushions
- Charging case (for earbuds)
- Headband padding (for over-ear)
- Cable (for wired models)
- Charging contacts

**Common wear patterns:**
- Ear cushion wear / compression (over-ear headphones)
- Eartip degradation
- Cable insulation wear near connectors (wired)
- Charging case lid hinge looseness

**Audio-specific disclosures:**
- Battery health per earbud and case (AirPods: Settings → Bluetooth → information button)
- Ear tip / cushion condition (replaced or original)
- Driver imbalance (one side louder than the other)
- Noise cancellation effectiveness (if reduced)

## 12. Common Grading Mistakes

These are the most frequent grading errors on Go2Hand, based on dispute patterns.

### Mistake 1: Inspecting in dim lighting

**Problem:** Scratches are almost invisible in dim or warm indoor lighting. Sellers grade A+ and buyers receive a B.  
**Fix:** Always inspect under a bright LED torch in a darkened room, in addition to normal room lighting.

---

### Mistake 2: Grading by feel, not criteria

**Problem:** "It looks pretty good to me" — subjective assessments drift toward optimism.  
**Fix:** Use this guide's criteria as a checklist. The fingernail test and 30 cm test are objective, not subjective.

---

### Mistake 3: Ignoring the back panel because "buyers mainly use the front"

**Problem:** Buyers physically hold and examine the entire device. Back panel condition matters.  
**Fix:** Grade the device holistically. A scratched back cannot achieve A+ regardless of screen condition.

---

### Mistake 4: Grading as A+ because the device has a case on it

**Problem:** The device under the case may have scratches from before the case was fitted.  
**Fix:** Always remove the case and inspect the bare device.

---

### Mistake 5: Not disclosing replaced components

**Problem:** A buyer receives a device with a non-original screen or battery and feels misled, even if the replacement was high quality.  
**Fix:** Always disclose replaced components. Non-original screens affect Face ID on iPhones — this is a significant functional change buyers must know about.

---

### Mistake 6: Listing water damage history as cosmetic-only

**Problem:** Water damage can cause delayed failure weeks after exposure, even if the device works perfectly at listing time.  
**Fix:** Always disclose any water exposure, even brief exposure. Use the language from [Section 10](#10-mandatory-disclosures-all-grades). Buyers can make an informed decision; hidden water history is deceptive.

---

### Mistake 7: Describing battery health without checking it

**Problem:** Estimating battery health ("it charges quickly, probably 90%+") leads to inaccurate listings.  
**Fix:** Always check the exact battery health percentage before listing and include the specific number.

---

### Mistake 8: Grading above actual condition to attract higher prices

**Problem:** This is the most common cause of disputes on Go2Hand. A buyer who pays Grade A prices for a Grade B device will dispute.  
**Fix:** Honest grading at the correct grade + an honest description builds trust that results in faster sales and better reviews. The short-term gain of an inflated grade is not worth the dispute risk and review damage.

## 13. Grading Examples with Photos

The following examples illustrate grade boundaries. When photographing your own device, aim for the same conditions: bright natural light or LED torch, clean lens, and clear focus.

### A+ Example — iPhone 15 Pro

**Description:** Zero scratches visible under torch, LED light, or natural sunlight. Corners perfect. Camera bump pristine. Screen flawless under all lighting. Original packaging included. Battery health 98%.  
**Verdict:** A+ ✅

### A Example — iPhone 14

**Description:** Under bright LED torch at 20° angle, 2–3 very faint micro-scratches on back glass, approximately 2mm each. Not visible in room lighting or from 30cm away. Screen flawless. One corner has the very beginning of edge wear — a slight lightening of the finish. Battery health 89%.  
**Verdict:** A ✅

### B Example — Samsung Galaxy S23

**Description:** Back panel has several light scratches visible in room lighting — most pronounced when the device is angled. Two corners show clear anodising wear with silver aluminium visible. Screen is clean and crack-free; faint surface marks visible only under direct torch. Battery health 81%.  
**Verdict:** B ✅

### B Example (with disclosure) — MacBook Air M2

**Description:** Palmrest shows significant anodising wear from keyboard use — particularly noticeable under the typing area. Display pristine. Keyboard all keys functional, legends clear. Bottom panel scratched from desk use. Battery health 83%.  
**Verdict:** B ✅ (palmrest wear is common on MacBooks and appropriate for B)

### C Example — iPhone 13

**Description:** Multiple visible scratches on back glass. Three corners show heavy anodising wear with aluminium clearly exposed. Small dent on bottom-left corner — does not affect chassis integrity or button function. Screen has light scratches visible in room lighting, no cracks. Battery health 74%.  
**Verdict:** C ✅

### Cannot-be-listed Example

**Description:** Screen cracked across centre affecting touch in a 3cm area. Speaker not functioning.  
**Verdict:** ❌ Cannot list on Go2Hand as a functional device. Must be listed as "for parts / repair" with full fault disclosure.

## 14. Dispute Prevention Checklist

Before publishing your listing, run through this checklist. Completing it significantly reduces the chance of a buyer dispute.

### Inspection checklist
- [ ] Device cleaned before inspection
- [ ] Screen inspected under LED torch at 20° angle
- [ ] Fingernail test conducted on screen and back
- [ ] 30 cm visibility test conducted
- [ ] All four corners and sides examined under torch
- [ ] All ports and buttons tested
- [ ] All camera lenses examined
- [ ] All functionality tested (see 15-point functional test in Section 7)
- [ ] Battery health percentage checked and recorded (exact number)
- [ ] iCloud / Google account removed completely
- [ ] Device factory reset

### Listing checklist
- [ ] Grade selected matches inspection results
- [ ] Battery health exact percentage included in description
- [ ] All mandatory disclosures included (see Section 10)
- [ ] At least 5 photos uploaded, including any wear areas
- [ ] Photos taken in natural light or under a bright LED
- [ ] Any cosmetic wear photographed specifically and clearly
- [ ] IMEI or serial number verified and result is "Clean"
- [ ] Listing title includes brand, model, storage, and colour

### Before shipping checklist
- [ ] iCloud / Google account removed (do this again — it is the #1 cause of disputes)
- [ ] Device factory reset
- [ ] Device photographed before packing (timestamp and packaging visible)
- [ ] Tracking number entered in Go2Hand dashboard before handing to courier
